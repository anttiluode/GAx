# Gate 5 — forecast before generation

## Question

Can a short history of already-observed GA generations predict a future generation **before that generation is allowed to exist**?

For the fixed infinite-population mutation-selection model,

\[
p_{g+1}=\frac{Lp_g}{\mathbf 1^\top Lp_g},
\qquad L=M\,\mathrm{diag}(G),
\]

the observed normalization factor

\[
z_g=\mathbf 1^\top Lp_g
\]

lets us reconstruct the hidden linear lift using only past observations:

\[
q_g=\left(\prod_{i<g}z_i\right)p_g.
\]

From the observed history

\[
q_0,q_1,\ldots,q_m,
\]

Gate 5 fits the minimum-norm linear map supported by that Krylov history,

\[
\widehat L=Y X^+,
\quad
X=[q_0\;\cdots\;q_{m-1}],
\quad
Y=[q_1\;\cdots\;q_m],
\]

then predicts

\[
\widehat q_{m+h}=\widehat L^h q_m
\]

and only afterward reveals the true future generated by the hidden `L`.

The fitted forecaster never receives the oracle operator `L`.

## What happened

The useful variable is not generation count by itself. It is the dimension of the **observed cyclic/Krylov subspace**.

With only two observed directions, the fitted operator is underdetermined away from the span it has seen and performs only slightly better than velocity at longer horizons. As the history contributes new independent Krylov directions, forecast error falls rapidly. In this symmetric 7-bit landscape the accessible trajectory reaches numerical rank 8. Once those eight directions have been observed, the history-fitted operator reproduces the future trajectory to floating-point-scale error even several generations ahead.

So, in this deterministic fixed-operator setting,

\[
\boxed{\text{past generations are a sensor of the hidden search operator on the cyclic subspace they excite}.}
\]

The result is stronger than retrospective explanation. The forecaster commits to an unrealized population first; the hidden GA then generates that population and provides the audit.

It also forecasts the decline of the weaker evolutionary branch, not merely the mean population velocity.

## Claim boundary

This gate does **not** establish that arbitrary practical GAs are forecastable this way.

It assumes:

- an infinite-population deterministic mutation-selection process;
- one fixed positive operator `L` over the forecast window;
- access to the normalization/mean-fitness factors already produced by past transitions;
- exact population distributions rather than finite samples;
- a low-dimensional cyclic subspace excited by the chosen initial population and symmetric landscape.

Finite populations introduce sampling noise. Crossover can make the population map nonlinear. Adaptive mutation, changing objectives and history-dependent search produce `L_g` rather than one fixed `L`. Those cases require a changing-operator predictor rather than this fixed Krylov fit.

## Why this matters for the later GAx branch

Gates 3–4 showed that population modes can correspond to separated strategies and even counterfactually distinct computations. Gate 5 returns to the original GAx question and supplies the missing forward-looking result.

The next bridge should therefore ask whether a short history can predict not only

\[
p_{g+h},
\]

but also a coarser future event such as

- which computational family will gain amplitude;
- when a weak family is about to become effectively unrecoverable;
- under slowly changing `L_g`, the next low-dimensional operator change or finite-time covariant subspace.

That should be tested without importing ThirdWay's rewrite machinery yet.
