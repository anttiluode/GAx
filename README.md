# GAx — Genetic Algorithm X

A mathematical exploration of a simple question:

> **What is the object formed by successive genetic-algorithm generations, if we treat the generations themselves as a dynamical signal?**

The first result is cleaner than the original metaphor.

For a finite genotype space, positive fitness weights `G`, and a Markov mutation matrix `M`, define

\[
L=M\,\mathrm{diag}(G).
\]

Then the normalized infinite-population GA is

\[
\boxed{p_{g+1}=\frac{Lp_g}{\mathbf 1^\top Lp_g}}.
\]

Lift to an unnormalized positive measure `q_g` and the generations become exactly linear:

\[
\boxed{q_{g+1}=Lq_g,\qquad q_g=L^gq_0.}
\]

So successive generations form a **Krylov trajectory of a positive operator**. The apparent nonlinear GA is a projective shadow of linear dynamics on the positive cone.

That is the starting object of GAx.

## Why this matters

It gives precise versions of several intuitions that otherwise sound metaphorical:

- generational history can contain information about unrealized future generations;
- more generations help only until the observed Krylov subspace saturates;
- convergence is governed by Perron–Frobenius spectral structure;
- long-lived competing branches correspond naturally to near-leading modes / metastability;
- adaptive search becomes a product of changing positive operators `L_g`, not merely a moving population mean;
- transfer across changed coordinates should preserve operator invariants/conjugacy, not raw direction vectors.

## Important correction: not "just Wasserstein"

Selection and mutation do different mathematical things.

A small exponential fitness reweighting gives

\[
\partial_t\rho=\rho(f-\bar f),
\]

a replicator/Fisher–Rao reaction. Small Gaussian mutation gives

\[
\partial_t\rho=D\Delta\rho,
\]

the Wasserstein heat flow. Together they produce a replicator–mutator reaction-diffusion equation, not in general the ordinary Fokker–Planck/JKO flow generated by moving particles along `grad f`.
